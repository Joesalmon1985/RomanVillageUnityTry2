## Decisions waiting on the owner

1. **Balance (the big one).** With the rules as written the island falls in about 69% of AI games, a war is on in about 55% of rounds and games end in about 16 rounds (targets: under 10%, under 30%, 25–40). `Docs/progress/G1-3-tuning.md` measures about 30 variants. The two-change option **F1** (infection draws every 3rd round instead of every round, and tied leaders never fight) gives 0% falls and 16% war share; adding **win at 13 points** (F3) or also **war lead 3** (D5) brings games to a median of 22–23 rounds with falls 4–6%. No option reaches a median of 25 rounds; that needs slower building (owner's call). No default was changed.
2. **WA-11 at the start:** read literally, everyone tied at the start is at war. Now a flag `WarState.TiedLeadersFight`, default "only when not everyone is tied"; a third value "never when tied" exists (used by F1).
3. **Fair starts (G0-1):** to reach 99% fair deals, once a deal gives good terrain for all four starts, later tries redeal only the numbers. Confirm.
4. **Ports (G0-1):** only 11 rim spots were allowed, so the 9 ports are unevenly spread (largest gap 106°).
5. **Plague details (G2-1):** a spread demon keeps its source's kind; a cured kind is never placed; banish uses one shared cooldown.
6. **Trading between AIs (G1-2):** TR-4 read literally means a leading AI only trades with the player.
7. **War details (G3-1):** war is mutual (the leader's army attacks back); home camps with empty stores are not targeted.
8. **Ledger:** table points are not fed into the old Ledger (it can't take points back when a settlement burns).
9. **Historic (G4-2):** the war keeps going after the switch but castles can't be attacked; the peoples share trees and rock with no borders; stone is the scarce good; the Historic module has its own small construction sites and carriers (the old economy only holds the four start camps on the god board).
10. **Campaign race (G2-3):** a player who stays at the board gets 0–2 crystals first; re-measure once the balance is set.
