## Done

New module `Assets/World/Sim/Historic/` (namespace `World.Sim.Historic`): `HistoricState` (+ `WorldState.Historic`, null until the age turns), `HistoricDefs` (building data), `HistoricEvents`, `HistoricRules` (switch, resources, buildings, carriers, castle), `HistoricPlanner` (the brain), `HistoricModule` (system, `Switch`), `HistoricApi` (queries for views and proofs).

**1. The switch (G1-8 sim part; HI-1, HI-2, HI-7, HI-8).** `GodBoardModule.AgeEnded` (the table's `OnAgeEnded` seam, and `CatchUpAgeEnded` for an age ended by a campaign grant) now ends the plague (unchanged, HI-8) and calls `HistoricModule.Switch` in the same tick: `ChapterReady(ch.historic)` as before, then `ErasLogic.Advance` to `ch.historic` (`ChapterAdvanced`, era Historic, history record), then `HistoricRules.Begin`: `HistoricState.TableClosed`, `HistoricBegan{Winner}`, `TemplesTornDown{Corners}` (each people's start corner, index = people), one `CastleStageStarted{Stage 0}` per people. The table was already `Ended` (commands refused with `table_closed`); nothing reopens it. A fallen island (`IslandFell`) never switches. The war is not touched (HI-7: it keeps running with its rules; the table's points stay frozen). Deterministic (no random numbers anywhere in the module) and saved. `HistoricModule.Register` is called from `GodBoardModule.Register`, so new and loaded god board games run the Historic system (slot `Production + 50` = 350; idle while `Historic` is null).

**2. Castles (HI-3, G4-1).** Each people's castle stands at its most productive table corner (highest summed pips, `BoardGenerator.CornerPips`; tie to the lowest corner; a people with no settlement left uses its start corner). That corner is also the people's Historic store. Three stages (wood, brick, stone), each a construction site fed by the people's carriers: `HistoricTuning.CastleCost` (stage 0: 30 wood; 1: 24 bricks + 12 wood; 2: 30 stone + 12 wood + 8 grain + 6 sheep), then `CastleStageTicks` (600) of building. Events `CastleStageStarted`, `CastleStageRaised` per stage; `Castle.RaisedTick` per stage.

**3. Finite resources (HI-5).** Per hex (`HexResources`, compact: one int per tree or field slot plus one tick per slot):
- Trees: Forest hexes have 12 slots, `2 + 2 x pips` grown trees at the switch; Meadow hexes 6 empty slots. Stages Empty, Sapling, Young, Mature, Stump. Woodcutters fell grown trees only (2 wood each), leaving a stump that rots after `StumpRotTicks` (900); trees come back only where a forester plants (Sapling to Young to Mature, `TreeGrowTicks` 600 per stage). Woodcutters and foresters work their own hex and its neighbours.
- Rock on Mountain (`10 + 6 x pips`) and Hills (`4 + 2 x pips`); a quarry takes one per stone and is `Exhausted` for good at zero (`QuarryExhausted`).
- Clay on Hills (`6 + 2 x pips`), dug by clay pits; it seeps back one unit per 300 ticks up to the start amount.
- Fields: `2 + pips` slots on Fields hexes; a farm sows (100 ticks), the field ripens in 900, the farm harvests 2 grain.
- Sheep: a sheep farm (Pasture or Meadow) turns 1 grain from a farm into 1 sheep.
- Kiln: 2 clay + 1 wood into 2 bricks.
- Production rolls stop: the table has ended, so there are no rolls; the dice clock was already off on the god board.
- New building kinds as data (`HistoricDefs`, `HistoricKind`): Woodcutter, Forester, Quarry, ClayPit, Farm, SheepFarm, Kiln (costs in wood, kiln 4 wood + 2 stone; 300 ticks to build).

**4. The Historic brain (HI-4, HI-6).** `HistoricPlanner.Plan` runs for every people (the player's included) every 50 ticks, staggered: one new site at most per plan, at most 2 open sites, by the first unmet need: a woodcutter; foresters ahead of woodcutters (one per woodcutter, one more when the young and grown trees in the woodcutters' reach run below 6 per woodcutter); a second woodcutter; a working quarry (an exhausted one is replaced); clay pit; kiln; farm; sheep farm; more woodcutters (up to 4) while wood is short and grown trees are scarce in reach; a fresh clay pit when ours stand on dry hexes and bricks are wanted; a second quarry while the castle wants stone. Sites are scored per kind (resources in reach, crowding, distance to the castle), ties to the lowest hex; the near territory (hexes touching the people's corners plus one ring) first, then a second ring. Up to 3 buildings per hex, shared by all peoples (they compete for trees). Written fresh after the Widelands computer player's ideas (needs, foresters ahead, replace exhausted mines); no GPL code.

**Carriers.** Each people has `3 + holdings` carriers (settlement 1, city 2). A carrier takes one unit: outputs to the store first, then construction goods to sites, inputs to workshops (two cycles' worth), then the castle, keeping 6 wood back for sites. Busy `30 + 40 x hex steps` between the building's hex and the castle corner. Ware books: `HistoricLedger` (Starting, Produced, Consumed) and `HistoricApi.Imbalance` (zero for every ware in every test and proof game).

**Not reused, and why.** The prehistoric economy's construction sites, carriers and grades (`Economy/Logistics.cs`, `Grades.cs`) work on economy settlements with plots and main roads; on the god board the economy has only the four start camps (table settlements and cities are not economy settlements; checked on seed 3000 at the age's end). Mirroring the table into economy settlements was too large for this package, so the Historic module has its own compact site and carrier mechanics with the same shape (delivered/incoming per ware, build ticks, one unit per carrier trip). The `ages/h1-5` building upgrades (grades) are not applied to Historic buildings. A view can still use the Historic models of h1-5 for castle stages.

**Save.** `SaveCodec.CurrentVersion` = 8. `WorldState.Historic` is plain data. Migration 7 to 8: a Legacy game gets `Historic = null`; a god board game whose table ended with a winner and a standing island (it was waiting at "Historic ready") steps into Historic at load (`HistoricModule.Switch` with no event buffer); other god board games stay as they were.

## Clay recovery (branch `godboard/historic-clay`)

**Cause.** With the Balance F1 defaults, seed 3003 people 1 stopped after the wood stage (`BALANCE_F1.md`). It had one clay pit, on a Hills hex shared with other peoples, and only two Hills hexes in reach. Two things starved it: (1) the planner only placed a clay pit on a hex holding at least 2 clay, so once both hexes were dry it could place nothing; (2) peoples that had already finished their castle kept digging and firing bricks (192 spare bricks at the end), draining the shared hex's slow seep (1 clay per 300 ticks per hex).

**Fix** (no change to the switch, the castle costs, or any other module).
- `HistoricPlanner.BestHex` for a clay pit: a hex with clay comes first (same choice as before), and when none is wet, any Hills hex in reach with a clay cap of 2 or more qualifies, shared or dry, because its clay seeps back. The existing "fresh pit while bricks are wanted, the store has no clay and our pits are dry" need (up to `MaxClayPits`) now finds a site.
- `HistoricRules.BricksStillWanted`: a clay pit idles once the people's store clay plus bricks (plus delivered and on the way) cover the bricks of its unfinished stages, and a kiln idles once the store bricks do; a finished castle needs none. Deterministic, uses only existing state, ledger unchanged and balanced.

**Proof.** `HistoricProof` asserts again that every people raises all three stages; `G4-2-proof.md` regenerated: 32 of 32 peoples with all three stages (was 31), median stone stage 13.4 min, worst 20.8 min (was 28.0), worst wood gap 1.7 min, ware books balanced in 8 of 8 games. Sweep of seeds 3000-3047: 47 games reach Historic, 188 peoples, 0 short of three stages, no wood gap over 5 min, all books balanced.

**Tests.** New in `HistoricTests`: dry hex still serves as a pit site (and a wet one is preferred), pits and kilns idle when the castle's bricks are covered, and seed 3003 raises all three stages for every people. See the end of this section for the full-suite count.
