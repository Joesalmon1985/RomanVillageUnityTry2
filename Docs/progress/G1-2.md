# G1-2 AI peoples: progress note

Branch `godboard/g1-2-ai`, worktree `wt-g12`, cut from the G1-1 merge (`9dcea4b`). Written 19 Nov 2026.

## What is done

New file `Assets/World/Sim/Table/TableAi.cs` (namespace `World.Sim.Table`): the AI brain, now the default on every `TableSeams` AI hook. The G1-1 stand-in stays: `TableSeams.Basic()` / `seams.UseBasic()` put `TableBasicAi` and the old defaults back (tests use them through `TableTestKit.IdleAi` and `TableTestKit.BasicAi`).

**Build brain.** `TableAi.TargetFor(state, people)` returns a `TableTarget` (Kind None/Road/Settlement/City, Spot to build next, Site the road heads for, Path of hex edges still to build, Cost). This is the call the player's adviser highlight (GB-7) can reuse.

- Sites are scored by `SiteScore`: 3 × summed pips + 2 × different goods + 3 × goods the people does not touch yet + port bonus (2 for a 3:1, 3 for a 2:1 of a good it touches), minus 4 per road still to build. This takes `BoardGenerator.SiteScore`'s idea that variety counts more than count.
- A 0-1 breadth-first search from the people's network finds sites up to `MaxRoadsToSite` = 3 roads away (own roads free, other peoples' roads and settlements block) and the road path; the first road on it is always legal.
- Cities: the settlement with the most pips. A city comes first when it has at least `CityFirstPips` = 10 pips, or when no site is left or no settlement piece is left. With nothing to settle and no city piece, a road for the longest road. Piece limits are respected (roads left cap the search; `None` when nothing can be built).
- The turn (`PlayTurn`): build the target when affordable, a city whenever affordable, else make one offer and wait for its answer, else bank or port.

**Trade brain (TR-4 to TR-7).**

- TR-4: `IsLeading` = highest points, ties included. `Refusal(s, ai, other)` = `leader_refuses` when `other` is leading. `Partners` = everyone not leading, sorted by lowest points, then lowest summed production pips (a city counts twice), then id. A leading AI still trades with non-leaders. Reading used (see "Open questions"): every AI side of a trade checks the other side, so two AIs only trade when neither leads; a leading AI can trade with a non-leading player.
- TR-5: `Wants` = the cards missing for the target, then goods it has no settlement next to (only traded for when holding more than `ShedAbove` = 7 cards); `Spare` = cards beyond the target's needs.
- TR-6: `Accepts` / `Fair`: not with a leader, can pay, progress to the target (missing cards) not lowered, at least as many cards in as out, except up to `LastCardPrice` = 2 for 1 when that 1 card completes the target.
- Offers: on its turn an AI offers 1 spare card for 1 wanted card (or 2-for-1 for the last card) to the most preferred partner not yet asked this turn, at most `MaxOffersPerTurn` = 3 a turn. AI partners are only asked when they would accept; the player (when not leading) is asked once a turn. Offers go to one people, never to everyone, so a leader can never pick them up. When no partner helps: bank or port (best rate, the spare good with most cards left over).
- Answers: the engine asks `AiAnswer` after `AiAnswerTicks` = 20 (unchanged), now `TableAi.Accepts`.
- TR-7: new event `TradeRefused { People, With, OfferId, Code, Sentence }`. When the leading player offers to everyone, each AI declines with this event ("They will not trade with the leader."); an offer addressed to one AI seat is refused at once with `leader_refuses`. AI-to-AI trades are one `Traded` event each (unchanged).

**Discards.** `TableAi.ChooseDiscard` discards from the good with the most cards beyond the target's cost first, so the target's cards go last. It is also the default for the player when their discard time runs out (the adviser's choice, TU-10).

**Setup.** `TableAi.SetupPlacement`: the legal corner with the best `SiteScore`, and the road whose far end leads to the best next site.

## Engine changes (not table rules; listed as asked)

All in `TableEngine.cs` / `TableState.cs` / `TableCommands.cs`:

1. `TableSeams.AiRefusal` hook (default `TableAi.Refusal`): `Check(OfferTrade)` refuses an offer addressed to an AI seat with its code; `AiAnswers` declines with a `TradeRefused` event. Needed for `leader_refuses` (declared in G1-1, unused until now).
2. AI turn replay: when an offer made by the current AI seat is answered or expires, `TableState.AiReplay` is set and `AiTurn` is called again on the next tick (at most `TableEngine.MaxAiCallsPerTurn` = 8 calls a turn), so the AI can build with the card it just got. New saved fields `AiReplay`, `AiCalls`, `AiAsked` (reset at each turn start).
3. An AI turn does not end while one of its own offers is still open (bounded by `OfferTicks` = 150), so the player gets the full offer time (TR-3) instead of the 30-40 ticks of an AI turn. The G1-1 test `AiOffersToPlayer_PlayerAccepts_OrItExpiresAtTurnEnd` was adjusted for this (its stand-in AI offers only on the first call; the unanswered offer now expires by time, then the turn ends).
4. Defaults of `ChooseDiscard`, `AiSetup`, `AiTurn`, `AiAnswer` are now the brain. G1-1 tests that relied on "AI declines" use `IdleAi`, which now also puts back the stand-in's answers; the G1-1 game tests run on `BasicAi` and keep their figures.

No rule bug was found; no table rule was changed.

## Proof figures (`Docs/progress/G1-2-proof.md`)

60 seeds (1000-1059), 4 AI seats, start corners 70, 97, 57, 82, `CreateMvpBoard(seed)`, no ports, no plague:

- All 60 games reach 10 points. Rounds: min 10, **median 16**, mean 16.2, max 26. **2 of 60 in the PRD band of 25-40 rounds: target not met.**
- Wins by seat: 23 / 12 / 7 / 18 (start corners 70 / 97 / 57 / 82).
- Trades between peoples: 677 (11.3 a game), all **677 obey TR-4 (100%)**. Bank trades 26 a game.
- With a Quick virtual player in seat 0 (same seeds): all end, median 15 rounds; AI offers to the non-leading player 9.08 per 3 rounds (target at least 1); 966 / 966 trades obey TR-4 on the AI side; the Quick player wins 0 of 60 (it never banks or offers, so it is a weak player, not a fair test of the 30-50% target; G1-3 owns that).

**Why the pace is short, and what was tried.** Pace barely depends on the AI: the G1-1 stand-in over the same 60 seeds gives median 16 rounds (10-29), wins 22/19/10/9. AI-field variants over 40 seeds (bank only above 5, 7 or 9 cards; 0, 1 or 3 offers a turn; cities first from 10 or 13 pips) all gave medians of 15-17 rounds. The game's length comes from the rules and board (start corners on Forest 6 / Hills 8 / Fields 5, two settlements at the start, no robber, no development cards, no plague yet). Reaching 25-40 rounds needs a rules or board decision (owner), for example: the plague's blocked hexes (G2-1) once wired, fewer pips at the start corners, or a higher `WinPoints`. No AI field was changed for pace; the experiment fields were not kept.

The win spread also leans on the start corners (seat 2 at corner 57 wins least with both AIs); that is G0-1's board deal to balance.
