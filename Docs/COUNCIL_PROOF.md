# Council proof (generated)

Generated by `CouncilProof` (Assets/World/Tests/EditMode/Council/CouncilProof.cs) under `dotnet test DotNet`; do not edit by hand. The same seeds always give this file byte for byte.

**These are automated simulations with scripted players. Nothing in this file is a human observation.** Every number says how the rules and the scripted players behave, not how people will play or feel. The human playtest must be reported separately.

Setup: the election rules in `ElectionEngine` (four candidates, tracks 2 to 12, three catspaws, three claims to win), the four shipped personalities in `council_content.json` dealt to seats by a seeded shuffle, and the listening rules: 5 responses, accuracy threshold 85%, speed bonus up to 5 points per correct answer (full at or under 1500 ms, none at or over 4000 ms), at most 2 bursts per election, one petition per election. A **sitting** is one candidate's turn; a **push** is one press-on; a **bust** is a push with no legal move.

## Known weaknesses

- **No humans.** Scripted listeners have a fixed chance of being right and a response time drawn at random; real response times, reading speed, fatigue and learning are not modelled, so the earn rates are a range, not a prediction. The threshold, the bonus and the burst length should be tuned from the human playtest.
- **Scripted decisions.** The player stops by a single exact bust-odds rule; people stop by feel, often too early or too late, and choose trays by what they can read on the table rather than by a score. Win rates of the scripted player say little about a person's.
- **First-mover advantage.** The candidate who starts wins 8.5 points more often than the one who plays last. The start is a fair seeded choice and seats rotate, so no seat is favoured over many games, but a single election starts tilted. The brief fixes the rule; a possible later tweak is a small head start for the last candidate.
- **Four fixed temperaments.** The rivals use four hand-tuned personalities; their win rates are close, not equal, and were tuned only by these simulations.
- **The petition is worth most to players who press on.** In these runs it moves a risk-taking scripted player's win rate by up to 14.0 points and barely moves a cautious one's, because it cancels one bust. If that is too strong for a side game, make it harder to earn (higher threshold, fewer bursts) rather than weaker; the rule that the sitting's progress is kept is fixed by the brief. Whether people press on more because they hold one is only visible in the "+10 points while holding" rows, which assume the answer.
- **Listening opportunities.** The simulation lets the player listen at the start of every rival sitting. In the game the player must look left in time, rivals' turns are fast, and a burst may be cut short; the real number of opportunities will be lower.
- **Same seeds, paired runs.** Rows share seeds, which makes differences cleaner but also makes the rows correlated; they are not independent samples.
- **Election length is in sittings and pushes, not minutes.** Real duration depends on animation and reading time, which the simulation does not have.
