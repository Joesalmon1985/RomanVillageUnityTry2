# World-first UI: audit and plan

Written 20 Nov 2026 on `godboard/ui-audit` (from `godboard/main` at `b93117a`). Analysis and plan only: no game code changed.

**Input.** The owner's world-first UI brief of 20 Nov 2026: one agent prompt, six functional layout wireframes (01 hub, 02 the hex board game, 03 the plague, 04 the council, 05 rhythm, 06 the crystal dungeon) and per-zone prompts for world objects and people. The brief lives **outside the repo** and must not be copied in: the repo is public and the brief's file names name commercial games. This document says it in our own words and never names those games. The wireframes are layout diagrams, not art: their colour blocks and labels must never appear in the game.

**Authority.** Where the brief and our working rules differ, **our rules win** and the difference is written down here (the brief asks for exactly that). Binding: `PRD_GODBOARD.md`; then the package notes in `Docs/progress/`, `COUNCIL_API.md`, `RHYTHM_API.md`, `WARD_API.md`, the campaign docs. `GODBOARD_UI.md` (G1-6b, 19 Nov) is **superseded where it conflicts** with this plan; section 4 says which parts of it survive.

Reading guide: 1 principles; 2 the six wireframes zone by zone; 3 the exact numbers and where they are read; 4 what happens to yesterday's HUD; 5 work packages for implementation agents; 6 questions for the owner.

## 1. The brief's principles, applied to our game

| # | Principle (our words) | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| P-1 | No permanent HUD: no standing button rows, floating numbers or icon menus. Each command and each piece of state has a home in the world: a person, a place, a prop, a behaviour. | The god board's edge HUD (G1-6b), the plague's "Plague" button and counter panel, the on-foot "Banish" button, the duel's weave panel and the dance's hidden HUD strip all move to world objects. Speech bubbles and reply lines stay: talking is in-world. |
| P-2 | Precision stays available. Where strategy needs an exact number, an object or person gives it on inspection (a ledger, a tally, a clerk). Animation alone is not enough. | Section 3 lists every number and its place. **AB-1 still rules:** no die face, number token, roll total or odds, ever, outside F1 tools. The council equally never shows its hidden values or the sums behind its places. |
| P-3 | One touch grammar everywhere: tap a thing or person to inspect or use it; tap the ground to move; drag empty space to look; **tap a proposal once to preview, again to confirm**; generous hit targets; a non-gesture alternative for every gesture. | We already have `IInspectable` / `IInteractable` (`Player/PlayerInterfaces.cs`) and `PointerInput`. Package WU-0 adds one shared preview / confirm / cancel / refused state on top. Keys stay as the alternative. |
| P-4 | Feedback is causal: people move, turn, argue, cheer; props change; sound and short effects back it up. A scoreboard only where accuracy or accessibility needs it. | Most of this exists in pieces (collecting villagers, council crowds, drums and dancers, epidemic sky, soldiers' wear). The gap is the edge HUD on the god board. |
| P-5 | State is authoritative; presentation follows it. A camera move, an interrupted animation or a fast double tap never repeats an action. | Already our rule: `Assets/World/Game` reads snapshots, sends commands and animates events. Council commands carry `ExpectedSeq`; the god board needs the same guard for build, trade and end turn (WU-2). |
| P-6 | Landscape phone first: safe areas, several aspect ratios, low-end Android, nothing important hidden behind another thing. | Our target stays 844 x 390 (about 19.5:9). Every package also captures 1920 x 1080 (16:9) and 1024 x 768 (4:3, the taller case), with a simulated notch safe area. |
| P-7 | The hub opens at night on one level green ground; each destination is a readable place you tap; there is no game-selection menu; every destination returns to the same hub view with state kept. | Our home camp already starts at night and every encounter restores the viewpoint (`EncounterHost`). Section 2.1 maps the six destinations onto our real places. |
| P-8 | Do not invent rules. Where a place has no game behind it yet, show an honest placeholder. | Our versions of the six games differ from the brief's assumptions in many places (section 2). We keep ours and never add mechanics to fill a wireframe zone. |
| P-9 | Editor-only debug outlines of the wireframe zones, off in builds. | WU-0 (section 5). |

**One thing the brief allows that we use:** for exact real-time play (stepping in the dungeon, hitting drums) a small fixed set of touch regions is acceptable, as long as they belong to the scene. Our four invisible touch strips (`Player/TouchStrips.cs`) are that.

**One thing our PRD allows that the brief would remove:** GB-3 says the god board is "the one place in normal play with on-screen controls". That permits on-screen controls; it does not require them. Moving them into the world (WU-2) is consistent with GB-3, and the HUD can remain as the accessibility layer (section 4).
