The real game adds walking and talk the model only approximates (straight-line walking, fixed talk), so the first playtest must measure real cast delays.
- Slow player (90 s): D, Dt, D, Dt, D, Dt, Dt, D, Dt, Dt. Decliner: V, V, V, V, Vt, V, Dt, Vt, Dt, Vt.
- Warriors barely appear: 0 of 10 prompt games train one before they end; meat is the rarest worked good (the Smokehouse needs a pasture camp and sheep also pay for camps).
- Strength is a count of camps, so games are mostly races to found camps; cities and warriors appear only in the longer games. Several prompt games end before the player builds its first workshop.
- The brain pursues one expansion target at a time and plans nothing else while three sites are open; a site that waits for a ware nobody collects blocks a slot until it is fed by an import.
- Logistics is hub-and-spoke with one unit per local trip; carriers are the bottleneck of a busy camp (tuned with faster carriers and more of them, not with bigger loads). Job ageing (after 30 s) overrides the serving order to stop starvation.
- Demons only draw on every third seven after 5 minutes (spec: every seven after 3 minutes): with the spec values the rival spent about half of its casts banishing and could not overrun an idle player.
- The press-the-advantage bonus (strength builds +30 while ahead of a people whose strength has not changed for 5 minutes) is what lets the rival overrun an idle player in time; it reads the other people's strength history, which a human cannot see.
- Shared dice give both start camps identical home numbers (6, 8, 5), so the first minutes are mirror images; differences come from the board beyond and from the cast delays.
